# Reduced physics model — material extension in version 0.4

**Status:** exploratory, unvalidated. This document describes the implemented reduced model and its intended validity boundary; `VALIDATION_STATUS.md` distinguishes numerical checks from experimental validation. References are indexed in `SOURCES.md`.

The app opens in a separate [Scene studio](SCENE_STUDIO.md). Its authored 8 m × 8 m Blender landscape, prescribed thermal palette and 20-second motion sequence are illustrative. They do not use the scientific state described here, report calculated temperatures or change the numerical trajectory. **Open simulation** enters this document's scientific workspace, whose default domain is 6.096 m × 6.096 m.

## Material configuration (version 0.4)

The optional `materialProperties` extension contains 34 independently validated coefficients. New scenarios include it; older schema-1 files retain their original material values and arithmetic/clamped peat-density mixing. New peat porosity uses additive specific volumes for dry organic/mineral mass fractions. Thermal storage, sublimation, gas viscosity, radial event limits and both mechanics models read the resolved scenario values. Changes restart all associated results. See [the evidence audit](MATERIAL_EVIDENCE_ASCE.md) and [composition profiles](RESEARCH_PROFILES.md) for values, bases, assumptions and scope. The equations below describe the same reduced model; quoted legacy coefficients are superseded by configured values when the extension is present.

## Domain and inventories

The surface is 6.096 m × 6.096 m (20 ft × 20 ft). The default depth is an editable 3 m demonstration value. Coordinates `x` and `y` lie along the surface; depth is positive downward. A 3D Cartesian grid has `nx × ny × nz` finite volumes; the fast default is 12 × 12 × 8. The selectable detailed grid uses `ceil(domain length / 0.1 m)` along each axis when within the 131,072-cell browser limit: 61 × 61 × 30 = 111,630 volumes for the default domain, with sides 9.993 cm × 9.993 cm × 10 cm. Larger domains may not support this preset within the browser limit. The 10 cm grid is a finer numerical discretization, not a claim of convergence or a material particle model. All solver lengths, masses, times, energies, pressures, and temperatures use SI units, with kelvin used inside temperature-dependent equations.

Per cell, the reduced state distinguishes dry combustible fuel and liquid water (kg), oxygen, CO₂, background gas, and water vapor (mol), temperature (K), and pore pressure (Pa). Solid/porous properties include porosity, intrinsic horizontal and vertical permeability, and moisture-dependent effective gas transport. Oxygen and CO₂ displayed as mole fractions are computed from gas mole inventories. Oxygen partial pressure is gas pressure times oxygen mole fraction; it is not synonymous with oxygen concentration.

Sand, silt, and clay fractions are dry *mineral mass* fractions that sum to one. The soil organic fraction is a fraction of total dry bulk mass. Ordered physical soil layers have thicknesses that sum to domain depth and modify base density, porosity, initial saturation, intrinsic permeability, and conductivity. Peat regions override local density, organic fraction, saturation, derived porosity, and conductivity after the layer adjustments; pathway multipliers apply afterward. Root amount is supplemental dry fuel mass per bulk soil volume, not a replacement for peat fuel; the decorative root lines do not themselves carry a separate numerical field. Moisture saturation is liquid-water volume divided by pore volume. It differs from the dry-mass-basis moisture content in Huang and Rein (S6).

The demonstration mixing rules are explicit and **uncalibrated**. Each finite volume is assigned a mineral, mixed mineral/organic, or peat matrix class from its center location and scenario regions; a matching layer supplies depth-dependent adjustments. The cell stores its resulting dry density, mineral and combustible mass, root fuel contribution, liquid water, porosity, conductivity, and horizontal/vertical permeability. Roots add fuel and pathways modify permeability without replacing the cell's matrix class. The scene offers a material-class overlay and the sensor reports the resolved properties. A class label is not an extra constitutive law. Soil intrinsic permeability is multiplied by `[(0.1 + 0.9 sand + 0.2 silt + 0.02 clay) / 0.623] × exp(−2 compaction)`, with the fractions on the mineral dry-mass basis. In peat cells, particle density is approximated by `1400 × organicFraction + 2650 × (1 − organicFraction)` kg/m³, porosity by `1 − dryBulkDensity/particleDensity` bounded to 0.1–0.9, and thermal conductivity by `0.16 + 0.6 × moistureSaturation` W/m/K. Peat cells also multiply the underlying horizontal and vertical intrinsic permeabilities by four and two, respectively. These coefficients are scenario assumptions, not sourced material correlations. A manually edited pathway multiplies intrinsic permeability inside its assumed geometry.

The gas-filled pore fraction is `fg = Vgas/(φ Vcell)`. Effective Darcy permeability is `kintrinsic × fg³` in each principal direction; effective gas diffusivity is `D0 × φ × fg² / tortuosity`. Intrinsic permeability itself remains fixed during a run. Dry fuel starts as `dryBulkDensity × organicFraction × cellVolume` plus one separately counted root contribution that decays with distance from the selected root depth and varies deterministically with the seed. This avoids including root fuel in the base organic fraction twice.

## Dry ice and heater

The source is buried directly in the soil. The Setup screen edits initial diameter `d₀` and solid density `ρₛ`; it derives initial mass `m₀` rather than offering a second mass input. The scenario schema stores that derived mass. They are linked by

```text
m₀ = ρₛ π d₀³ / 6,       d₀ = (6 m₀ / (π ρₛ))^(1/3).
```

The shrinking display uses the same volume equivalence for remaining mass. It does not resolve the true solid interface, cavities, or changing soil contact. Center burial depth and top cover depth are distinct: `cover = center depth − d₀/2`.

The principal heater input is volumetric generation `q'''` in W/m³ over a **fixed numerical support sphere** of radius `rₕ`; scheduled total power is `P = q''' × (4πrₕ³/3)` while enabled and within its time window. This fixed support volume prevents a changing dry-ice diameter from silently changing prescribed power. Heater energy is the time integral of `P`. The source energy balance must account for heater input, contact heat exchange, sensible heating of remaining solid, and the latent cost of sublimated mass without counting the same heater energy twice.

Source/soil contact heat uses an assumed areal conductance times the surface area of the *current equivalent sphere* and the local soil-to-source temperature difference. That surface-area law and the 850 J/kg/K solid heat capacity are demonstration assumptions. If the source starts below 194.65 K, available energy first warms it toward that set temperature; at or above it, available energy can sublimate finite mass. Gas added at the source cools surrounding soil through a separate approximate sensible-heat term. If the inventory exhausts partway through a step, unused heater/source energy returns to the local soil cells; later heater input goes directly to those cells. The cumulative source ledger tracks heater input, soil exchange, solid sensible energy, latent energy, and returned excess.

The solver uses 571 kJ/kg for CO₂ sublimation near its 194.65 K set temperature, a rounded approximation to the NIST WebBook value of 25.2 kJ/mol at 195 K (about 573 kJ/kg, S1). A constant latent heat is a coarse approximation. NIST reports the CO₂ triple point at 216.58 K and 5.185 bar; the solid-to-gas-only source treatment does not cover a liquid phase or a high-pressure release. Initial solid temperature is limited to at most the fixed 194.65 K sublimation temperature, and the gas pressure guard remains within 80–150 kPa. Source inventory is finite; exhausted dry ice contributes no further CO₂, while existing gas and heat continue to move.

The Event screen also exposes an explicitly hypothetical, one-time **convert remaining solid CO₂ to gas now** operation. At unchanged solver time it transfers all remaining solid mass into CO₂ moles at the source support cells, sets remaining solid mass to zero, and records an operational event. The model books the estimated solid sensible warming, 571 kJ/kg latent cost, and gas warming to local soil temperature as *external intervention energy*; it does not withdraw that energy from the soil. The conversion then recomputes ideal-gas pore pressure. If pressure exceeds the supported slow-flow range, converted mass remains in the state but subsequent transport pauses before a Darcy step. This mass-transfer tool does not model the rate, hardware, pressure wave, blast, or soil movement of a real release.

An additional **pressure-load display proxy** uses near-source excess pore pressure `Δp = max[0, Σᵢ(wᵢ pᵢ) − p_atmosphere]`, where the `wᵢ` are the source's trilinear cell weights. It multiplies this by the area of an *imagined fixed support plane*, `A = π rₕ²`, to show `F_proxy = Δp A` in newtons. The selected heater/support radius `rₕ` sets this area even after the solid mass reaches zero. This is an algebraic pressure-times-area calculation, not a stress integration on a real interface, measured uplift force, soil failure threshold, or blast force. Its status is `within-reduced-model` while the slow-flow calculation is running and `outside-validity` after a validity/numerical pause. An out-of-range number remains visible only as arithmetic from the retained state, not as a supported physical prediction (S8).

## Combustion and water

### Fire milestone extension on `feature/predictive-soil-fire-physics`

The implemented reaction remains a **single, complete oxidative surrogate** for dry organic fuel. It is not a calibrated multistep peat/char scheme. Drying is an independently accounted evaporation process; nonoxidative fuel decomposition and char oxidation are not included. The new state keeps nonreacting mineral mass (kg/cell) separate from organic fuel and tracks supplemental root fuel (kg/cell) as an explicit subset of the total fuel. Root and nonroot organic fuel are consumed in proportion to their remaining masses under the same surrogate rate; this is a numerical mixture assumption, not a measured root kinetic law. Minerals neither burn nor disappear. The dry bulk-density property remains fixed during this fire-only mode, so mechanical support after burnout is **not** calculated.

Each accepted step stores the actual local oxidation rate in kg/s/cell and its heat source in W/m³ of bulk cell volume. A hot cell with zero oxidative reaction shows zero activity. The field overlay displays activity independently of temperature. A change of atmospheric O₂ mole fraction is a prescribed boundary event; it leaves the interior gas inventory unchanged until transport evolves it. For a separate inhibition/rebound benchmark, `setUniformOxygenFraction` replaces O₂ moles with equal background-gas moles at fixed total moles and temperature. Its event records signed external O₂ and background moles, and zero external energy under the solver's equal per-mole gas heat-capacity approximation. This is an imposed mixture exchange, **not** a CO₂/dry-ice prediction or physical injection design. After restoring O₂, oxidation can resume if fuel and temperature remain available; no irreversible extinguished flag is set. Cooling and depletion can instead prevent renewed activity. The treatment outcome depends on the chosen numerical state and is unvalidated.

The published 30 cm peat-column study by [Huang and Rein (2017)](https://connectsci.au/wf/article/26/11/907/194558/Downward-spread-of-smouldering-peat-fire-the-role) uses measured peat properties, a different ignition protocol, and a multistep reaction model. The current 6.096 m demonstration with assumed kinetics does not match its geometry/material basis and must not be called a reproduced spread-rate benchmark. The [Gpyro Workbook v3](https://zenodo.org/records/13832584) identifies worked smoldering cases, but none has yet been matched with this solver. Independent experimental validation remains blocked on matched kinetic/property and boundary data.

The version 0.1 fuel surrogate uses the mass-balanced complete-oxidation reaction

```text
C₆H₁₀O₅ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 5 H₂O(vapor).
```

It consumes finite dry fuel and oxygen, produces CO₂ and water vapor, and adds `reacted fuel mass × heatOfCombustionJkg` to the cell energy balance. The reaction rate per unit remaining fuel is `kref exp[−Ea/R × (1/T − 1/Tref)] × xO2/(xO2 + K_O2)` above a selected minimum temperature, with per-step conversion capped by available fuel and oxygen. Every kinetic coefficient is a **reduced, uncalibrated demonstration value**, not peat-specific chemistry or a measured rate law. No char, ash, CO, or intermediate pyrolysis inventory is generated. Initial hot-region temperature seeds the state; it is not maintained as a thermostat. The on-screen peat glow follows solver temperature and creates no heat. Zero oxygen prevents this oxidation reaction, but does not disable conduction or source heat transfer. CO₂ reaching hot peat can lower local oxygen mole fraction and alter heat transport; it is not a direct extinguishing switch.

The solver reports cumulative reaction heat `Σ(m_reacted × heatOfCombustionJkg)` in joules, last accepted-step reaction power `Q_step/Δt` in watts, and the count of cells with positive oxidation in that accepted step. These quantities are restored with checkpoints and rolled back when a step fails. The broader, drier default hot peat region makes reaction heat visible in this demonstration, yet the finite hot spot can still cool when heat losses exceed reaction heat. This is not a calibrated claim of sustained peat combustion.

Liquid water increases the local heat capacity by 4186 J/kg/K by default (4180 in legacy files), consumes approximately 2.26 MJ/kg when evaporated, adds water vapor to gas storage, and reduces connected gas-pore transport through the `fg` rules above. Evaporation uses a first-order rate ramped from an onset temperature to a boiling-temperature setting and capped by available sensible energy; it is not a vapor-liquid equilibrium calculation. The model does not solve liquid-water flow, capillary redistribution, condensation, freezing/thawing, or swelling. It rejects an initially frozen wet scenario and rolls back and pauses a step that would cool liquid-bearing soil below 273.15 K; dry cells may remain colder. This is a validity guard, not a latent-freezing calculation. A single moisture setting cannot be interpreted as a universal reduction in peat-fire spread: S6 observed the opposite trend for downward spread in a particular 30 cm peat-column experiment as dry density and oxygen supply changed.

## Gas and heat transport

The reduced gas mixture is treated as an ideal gas in the available gas-filled pore volume. Storage uses `p Vg = ntot R T`, where `Vg = φ(1 − Sw)Vcell`, `φ` is porosity and `Sw` is liquid saturation. Pressure-driven transport approximates Darcy velocity `u = −keff/μ ∇p` using fixed mixture viscosity `μ = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ Pa s`; mole-fraction diffusion uses `Deff`. No buoyancy or variable-viscosity calculation is included. The configured atmosphere composition initializes the gas field and sets composition at open boundaries; a distinct interior initial-gas composition is not yet configurable. The open top exchanges gases with the atmosphere. A homogeneous sphere does not create a directed jet: anisotropy and preferential pathways must be explicit assumptions in the scenario.

Heat moves by conduction between cells and through top/bottom Newton-type exchange. Local sources and sinks are oxidation heat, prescribed heater input, source/soil exchange, and evaporation/sublimation energy. The computational side and bottom boundaries are model choices, not physical walls around an actual fire. The default side gas boundary is no-flux; an atmospheric side alternative exists. The bottom gas boundary is no-flux. The side thermal boundary is insulated, while the bottom exchanges heat with a prescribed deep-ground temperature. Top gas composition and heat exchange use configurable atmospheric conditions. These boundaries should be varied in sensitivity studies.

## Numerical method and diagnostics

The renderer's simulation worker advances physical solver time independent of wall-clock time, including inside the packaged Mac app. UI playback or time-lapse of recorded snapshots does not alter the time step of the numerical solution. A finite-volume balance sums face fluxes and local sources per cell. Heat and CO₂ from the small source are apportioned to the nearest eight cells with trilinear weights; its fixed *heater* support radius defines prescribed power, while the shrinking equivalent sphere determines soil-contact area. The support distribution is a subgrid approximation, not resolved cavity geometry.

Intercell heat conduction uses harmonic face conductivity and explicit energy updates. The pressure field solves an implicit linearized pore-storage/Darcy relation with **symmetric Gauss–Seidel-preconditioned conjugate gradients**. Its matrix-free operator directly applies a seven-point stencil using the local diagonal and three positive-direction conductance arrays; there is no dense global pressure matrix. Forward and backward ordered sweeps apply the symmetric preconditioner `M = (D + L) D⁻¹ (D + U)`. The iterative target is `1e-5 Pa` in diagonal-scaled residual units. After iteration, the code recomputes the actual equation residual, divides each cell's residual by its diagonal and takes the maximum absolute value. A nonfinite value or one above **0.05 Pa-equivalent** pauses the step; this acceptance gate is distinct from the tighter iterative target. These tolerances measure the numerical equation, not physical calibration.

Molar flow uses a fixed gas viscosity and a near-atmospheric molar density. Species transport uses upwind advection and mole-fraction diffusion; advective substeps limit outgoing cell-gas turnover to about 20% per substep, with a numerical pause if over 200 substeps would be required. The pressure solve is not a full compressible multicomponent CFD method. A step is bounded by the configured maximum, heater start/stop events, and the conservative explicit 3D bound `0.4/[max(α, D)(1/Δx² + 1/Δy² + 1/Δz²)]`, using the largest cell thermal diffusivity `α` and effective gas diffusivity `D`. An additional source-depletion limit bounds each step to at most roughly half the current latent-energy inventory at the estimated incoming power. The worker advances in bounded chunks at a requested pace of 30, 120, 600, or 3600 simulated seconds per wall second and reports the achieved pace; requested fast-forward never multiplies the explicit step. Paused or throttled background tabs do not accrue missed wall time. Recorded playback follows saved physical timestamps at a separate selected pace and does not advance the solver.

Diagnostics include gas inventory correction, fuel/water correction, gas balance residual, **separate oxygen/CO₂/background/vapor mole residuals**, source energy residual, resolved heat residual, cumulative CO₂ input/outflow, oxygen influx, fuel consumption, reaction heat/power/reacting-cell count, and evaporation. Each species ledger integrates initial moles, signed local sources (reaction, evaporation, dry ice, or a recorded benchmark exchange), signed boundary outflow, and numerical positivity correction. Component fluxes and corrections are checkpointed and rolled back with failed steps; older checkpoints without them begin component accounting at restart while retaining their original whole-gas ledger. New checkpoints reject altered species inventories. A finite residual is a numerical accounting measure, not validation against field measurements. Any nonzero corrective clipping should be visible in the correction diagnostics. A failed validity or numerical step restores the last accepted state and pauses with a warning; pressure and Darcy-velocity limits define the supported slow-flow window. Serialized checkpoints reject negative inventories and gas states inconsistent with their conservation ledger.

## Separate mechanics milestone and unresolved effects

The optional short-time radial gas event reads a 3D snapshot, often after the conversion, and evolves a separate, bounded 0–2 s pressure/CO₂ shell calculation with an uncalibrated effective-stress yield/damage indicator. Its pressure frames now load an independent worker that calculates reduced vertical soil force balance, gravity, elastic displacement, and tensile yield at 4³–8³ resolution. Neither model extends the slow Darcy equations into a blast solver or feeds damage/deformation back into the multiday grid. The gas event's clock, equations, 5 MPa/50 m/s numerical bounds, and limitations are documented in [`FAST_EVENT_MODEL.md`](FAST_EVENT_MODEL.md). Mechanics equations, pressure mapping, assumptions, boundaries, and checks are in [`MECHANICS_MODEL.md`](MECHANICS_MODEL.md).

The manually triggered opening sequence and crack lines remain **illustrative**. Short-event shell yield/damage values are also illustrative indicators. The new mechanics elements move according to calculated vertical displacement and mark a separate yielded region, using assumed stiffness/strength; they do not resolve fracture surfaces or conserve a deforming porous mesh. A pathway edited after an illustration is an **assumed hypothetical geometry** for a separate run. The app does not model blast propagation, calibrated field displacement, pressure containment, source-scale contact resistance, CO₂ dissolution, liquid CO₂, or validated poromechanical feedback. Smoke and steam appearance uses modeled oxidation/water-loss source rates with approximate atmospheric condensation and visual plume paths; CO₂ is invisible.
